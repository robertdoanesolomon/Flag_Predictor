How good is it?

Tested on every year from 1993/94 to 2025/26. Each year was forecast only from the other years, from the 1st and 15th of every month. The score compares the outlook with assuming an average year: 100% is perfect, 0% is no better than an average year, and below 0% is worse.

How it works

Flags from flow. The flag follows Farmoor's flow closely. Using the mean of today and yesterday, the Isis changes at m³/s (Green, Blue, Amber, Red; Light and Dark Blue are merged and Black counts as Red) and Godstow at m³/s (Green, Amber, Red). These lines give the OURCs flag on of days since 2019, so they can rebuild the flag for every day back to 1992.

The outlook. Each past year since 1993 gives one possible future: that year's rain from today's date on, started from today's river. The river's memory comes from the last 7 days (how high it is now) and the last 90 days (how wet the ground and groundwater are). The size of that effect, for each day ahead, is learned from the past years. The further ahead, the more the outlook relaxes to an average year.

What it cannot do. Nobody can forecast the weather months ahead, so one wet month can undo a dry outlook (as in Hilary 2026). Next step: testing whether ECMWF's seasonal rainfall forecasts add anything.
