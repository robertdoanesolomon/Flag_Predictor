Flag Predictor

A 10-day forecast of the river height differential and OURCs flags on the Isis, Godstow and Wallingford stretches of the Thames, updated hourly. Experimental.

The chance comes from the OURCs flag history: flags change almost only in daylight, go up within an hour or two of the differential crossing a line, and come down later, once it is clearly below the line, so the flag can lag the differential.
  • Black flag (Isis): a card appears only when Black is close, from a forecast of the Iffley Lock level.
  • Lock-keeper jumps: for the first three days the forecast moves in held levels and daytime jumps, as the weirs are adjusted, instead of a smooth line. Each forecast outcome is reshaped, not changed: jumps come mostly mid-morning to early afternoon, bigger when the river has further to go, so this sharpens when a band changes, not how high the river gets.
  • Gauge outages: a stretch whose gauges are offline shows no flag-change forecast; when they come back, the gap is bridged.

About these forecasts

Reading the page

The big tile is the official OURCs flag, from ourcs.co.uk. The cards give the chance it changes today or by the end of tomorrow, and where the differential is heading. In the chart, the black line is the measured differential; each thin purple line is one possible future from a different rain forecast, the bold line is their average, and the shading is the likely range (rain and model error together; outer 80%, inner 50%). The chart below it gives the chance of each band over the next 10 days.

The flag and the differential

The coloured bands are ranges of the differential that roughly match the flags, but OURCs sets the flag by hand, so the two agree only about 80–87% of the time. Flags go up within an hour or two of the differential crossing a line, come down later once it is clearly below, and almost only change between 8am and 8pm; the flag card's chances come from a model fitted to the OURCs flag history. For the first three days the forecast moves in held levels and daytime jumps, as lock keepers adjust the weirs, rather than a smooth line. Black (Isis) is forecast from the Iffley Lock level (Black from about 0.42 m, back to Red below 0.22 m), and its card appears only when Black is close.

How good is it?

Treat it as experimental. Checked on years it was not trained on, cards saying 80% or more came true about 9 times in 10, and 60–80% about 6 to 7 times in 10. Under each chart you can compare any recent forecast with what the river then did. The model cannot rise without rain, but it can overshoot after heavy rain and still misses some weir changes. The summary sentence is written automatically from the average forecast.

Data and updates

River levels and rain from the Environment Agency; rain forecasts from ECMWF AIFS (50 members) via Open-Meteo. The model is a physics-based rainfall–runoff model whose starting state is set by a small neural network, averaged over five trained copies. It runs hourly; if a gauge has been offline for more than four hours the forecast is marked stale and no flag-change forecast is shown. Write-ups are on the substack; bug reports and ideas are welcome on GitHub.
